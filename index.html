About me

Most people spend most of their working hours on work they don't care about. The forms, the follow-ups, the copy-paste between systems. The work they actually came to do gets whatever is left.

For the first time in history, that's fixable. AI can take the busywork off a person's plate at a scale no technology before it could. I want to build the tools that do it, so people can spend their lives on the work they care about.

I didn't come up through CS. I came up through construction, and I learned this problem firsthand.

At Paramount Granite, I ran 50+ contractor accounts and up to 30 active jobs at once. Quotes, slab orders, installs, every status in a CRM. The craft was in the stone. The hours went to the paperwork.

So I taught myself React and TypeScript and built a countertop quoting wizard that turns a hand sketch into a priced quote. Custom shapes, polygon math, AI sketch extraction. That was the moment I knew what I wanted to do with my life.

Every project since has pulled on the same thread. CRM-Bench measures whether AI agents can actually do real business work yet. TDFeed turns hours of keeping up with research into minutes. My job-search pipeline does the grind of finding and filling applications so I can spend my time on the ones that matter.

Today I'm leading the full website redesign at Wallin Education Partners, which helps students from lower-income backgrounds get to and through college, so they can build careers they choose. Same mission, different scale.

I'm finishing my B.S. in Software Engineering at the University of Minnesota (May 2027, 4.0 GPA) and moving to San Francisco to build this full time.

I believe people should own their time. Mindset beats odds. Know thyself. AI is the lever, not the point. The point is the person using it.

My goal is simple to say and hard to do: help millions of people spend their days on work they're proud of.

Origins

I grew up in Minnesota. My first real pitch was for my high school robotics team, M-TECH: I cold-called local companies and raised $4,000 in sponsorships in two months. We used it to run robotics workshops for elementary kids.

Nobody handed me a path into tech. I cold-called, taught myself, and built my way in. I believe that's open to anyone, and I want to build the tools that make it easier.

Luck is mostly showing up again.
